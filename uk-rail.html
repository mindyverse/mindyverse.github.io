Power BI portfolio case study

UK Rail Ticket Sales & Journey Outcomes Analysis

A small end-to-end analysis of 31,653 mock UK National Rail ticket transactions, focused on purchasing patterns, gross ticket sales and the journey outcomes attached to those ticket records.

View GitHub Repository
Project purpose

What I wanted to understand

I wanted to understand ticket purchasing patterns, gross ticket sales and journey outcomes using a dataset that was small enough to complete carefully but large enough to practise a real Power BI workflow.

My process

From raw CSV to a dashboard I could defend.

01 • Audited the data

I checked data types, missing values, duplicates, category consistency and whether blanks made sense in the business context.

02 • Cleaned carefully

I standardised inconsistent delay-reason labels and kept legitimate blanks instead of deleting them automatically.

03 • Built the analysis

I created DAX measures and visuals for ticket sales, routes, journey outcomes, disruption causes, purchase channels and monthly sales.

Key findings

What the dataset showed

  • Advance tickets generated the highest gross ticket sales: £309,274.
  • Online purchases accounted for 18,521 transactions compared with 13,132 station purchases.
  • Manchester Piccadilly → Liverpool Lime Street was the most frequently recorded route, with 4,628 transactions.
  • Weather was the most frequently recorded disruption cause after category standardisation.
  • There were 1,118 refund requests among 4,172 disrupted ticket records.
What I learned

The important part was not just the charts.

The project helped me practise checking whether a metric was genuinely supported by the data before using it. For example, I used “gross ticket sales” rather than profit or net revenue because the dataset does not contain operating costs or confirmed refund payments.

The source is mock UK National Rail data. The results are portfolio analysis and should not be interpreted as official National Rail performance statistics.
